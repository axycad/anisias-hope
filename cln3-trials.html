Past studies and what they found

Treatment studies that have finished, stopped or never started, with what was reported. Small, uncontrolled studies cannot show whether a treatment works. We quote the authors' own conclusions and link to the source.

  • Stem cell transplant for inborn errors of metabolism (1995 to 2010). Sponsor: University of Minnesota. Registry: NCT00176904.
    A long running transplant study covering many inherited metabolic conditions, including juvenile NCL. Results are posted on the registry but are not reported separately for CLN3.
    What was published: No CLN3 specific outcome has been published from this study that we could find (registry results).
  • Mycophenolate (CellCept) (2011 to 2015). Sponsor: University of Rochester. Registry: NCT01399047.
    A placebo controlled crossover study of an immune suppressing medicine in 19 people with CLN3.
    What was published: Short term mycophenolate was well tolerated. The study found no definite effect on markers of autoimmunity or on clinical measures over the study period (PMID 29923092, JIMD Reports, 2019).
  • Optic to audio device (OrCam MyEye 2) (2021 to 2023). Sponsor: NICHD. Registry: NCT04974307.
    A pilot of a wearable device that reads text and recognises objects aloud. The registry reason for ending early is low enrolment.
    What was published: Ten participants were assessed, nine with CLN3. One minor adverse event was recorded. The authors report the device was safe and practical for most participants and call for longer studies (PMID 41933359, Orphanet Journal of Rare Diseases, 2026).
  • Miglustat (Batten 1) phase 1/2 (2022 to 2024). Sponsor: Beyond Batten Disease Foundation. Registry: NCT05174039.
    An open label safety and dosing study of oral miglustat in six people with CLN3. A phase 3 study has since been registered (NCT07882290, listed above).
    What was published: Results are posted on the registry: the main measure was treatment emergent adverse events; one of six participants had a serious adverse event and there were no deaths (registry results). We found no journal paper from this study yet.
  • PLX-200 (gemfibrozil) phase 3 (registered 2020, withdrawn). Sponsor: Polaryx Therapeutics. Registry: NCT04637282.
    A planned phase 3 study that never started. The registry reason is that the study had yet to begin and was removed to avoid further outreach to the sponsor.
    What was published: No results, because no one took part.
  • Cord blood transplant in NCL (outside a CLN specific trial) (reported 2025). Sponsor: Duke University. Registry: Not a registered trial.
    A retrospective report of eight children with NCL who received umbilical cord blood transplants at one US centre, four of them within a wider registered study of many conditions (NCT02254863).
    What was published: No deaths were related to the transplant procedure. Two children with CLN2 died during follow up, and three of four children with CLN3 had stable Hamburg scores over the period reported. This was a small case series without controls (PMID 39471475, Stem Cells Translational Medicine, 2025).

How we source listings

On 3 November 2026 we searched ClinicalTrials.gov, the EU Clinical Trials Information System (CTIS), the EU Clinical Trials Register and the ISRCTN registry by condition name, gene name and older names for each form (for example late infantile NCL for CLN2 and juvenile NCL for CLN3). Each entry links to its registry record. We list what the registry says, flag records that have not been updated for more than two years, and note known stops reported by sponsors. We do not repeat company claims about results.

We could not search WHO ICTRP, ANZCTR (Australia and New Zealand), ChiCTR (China) and jRCT (Japan) automatically on 3 November 2026, because the sites blocked automated searches or did not return results. Studies in those regions that are also on ClinicalTrials.gov are included above.

Search tip

You can search public registries yourself, and it is best to discuss anything you find with your clinical team:

  • ClinicalTrials.gov: try “Batten”, “neuronal ceroid lipofuscinosis” or “CLN3”.
  • EU CTIS: trials in the European Union since 2023.
  • NIHR Be Part of Research: UK studies, with the option to register your interest.
  • WHO ICTRP: brings together registries from many countries.

This tip is general guidance only. It is not medical advice.
