Open trials for CLN9

There are no trials for CLN9, because it is no longer a separate condition. This page explains where to look instead.

Registry check Last reviewed: 3 November 2026

Listing a study is not an endorsement. Only your clinical team can say whether a study is suitable for you or your child. Please talk to them before contacting any study.

Studies listed worldwide

None. CLN9 is a historical label and no study on ClinicalTrials.gov, the EU Clinical Trials Information System (CTIS), the EU Clinical Trials Register and the ISRCTN registry registers it as a condition (checked 3 November 2026). If a family once told "CLN9" now has a confirmed diagnosis, see the trials page for that form, for example CLN5 or CLN2.

How we source listings

On 3 November 2026 we searched ClinicalTrials.gov, the EU Clinical Trials Information System (CTIS), the EU Clinical Trials Register and the ISRCTN registry by condition name, gene name and older names for each form (for example late infantile NCL for CLN2 and juvenile NCL for CLN3). Each entry links to its registry record. We list what the registry says, flag records that have not been updated for more than two years, and note known stops reported by sponsors. We do not repeat company claims about results.

We could not search WHO ICTRP, ANZCTR (Australia and New Zealand), ChiCTR (China) and jRCT (Japan) automatically on 3 November 2026, because the sites blocked automated searches or did not return results. Studies in those regions that are also on ClinicalTrials.gov are included above.

Search tip

You can search public registries yourself, and it is best to discuss anything you find with your clinical team:

  • ClinicalTrials.gov: try “Batten”, “neuronal ceroid lipofuscinosis” or “CLN9”.
  • EU CTIS: trials in the European Union since 2023.
  • NIHR Be Part of Research: UK studies, with the option to register your interest.
  • WHO ICTRP: brings together registries from many countries.

This tip is general guidance only. It is not medical advice.
