Frequently asked questions

Short, honest answers for families and supporters. We do not give medical advice — your clinical team comes first.

What is Batten disease?

Batten disease is a group of rare, inherited conditions that affect the brain and nervous system. It usually starts in childhood. Over time, children and young people can lose skills such as vision, speech, movement, and thinking — though every person’s journey is different.

Clinically, these conditions are also called neuronal ceroid lipofuscinoses (NCLs). Different genetic forms are often labelled CLN1–CLN14. Anisia's Hope exists for families facing any of these forms.

What happens after diagnosis?

In general — and this is not medical advice — families are usually guided first by their clinical team (for example neurology, metabolic, genetics, or a specialist clinic). They can explain tests, follow-up, and any treatments or trials that may be relevant to your child’s specific form of the disease.

Alongside clinical care, many families look for clear information, practical help with daily life, and connection with others who understand. We can walk with you on that non-medical side; we cannot replace your doctors.

How can Anisia's Hope help right now?

We are a newly registered CIO. Right now we can:

  • Listen and respond to emails from families — we aim to reply within a few working days
  • Point you to our newly diagnosed page and this FAQ
  • Share careful signposts to other organisations and public NHS information families often find useful
  • Keep building support, research-grant, and education pathways (see our aims on the About page)

Email info@fightingbattendisease.org and tell us what would help.

Do you give medical advice?

No. Anisia's Hope does not give medical advice. Always speak to a qualified clinician about diagnosis, treatment, and care.
